A board-ready dashboard connects a small set of reliable measures to the decisions leadership needs to make. For a recurring-revenue business, that usually means explaining revenue growth, retention, acquisition efficiency, and cash assumptions together. A single ARR number cannot explain the quality of growth.

This is a working framework, not a report of a client engagement. The definitions should be adapted to the business model and reconciled with the organisation's finance records.

## Start with the decision

Write the decision at the top of the board paper. It might concern hiring, a channel investment, a pricing change, a fundraising timeline, or a response to customer churn. State the recommendation, the alternatives, the evidence, and what would change the recommendation.

Then choose the measures needed to assess that decision. Operational detail can sit in an appendix. The summary should allow a reader to understand what changed, why it matters, and what action is requested.

## Define the recurring-revenue metrics

| Measure                 | Working definition                                                                          | What to document                                                   |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| MRR                     | Monthly recurring revenue at a defined point in time                                        | Contract treatment, currency, discounts, and exclusions            |
| ARR                     | Annualised recurring revenue; often 12 × MRR for monthly reporting                          | Revenue model and annualisation method; separate one-off revenue   |
| ARR growth              | (Ending ARR − starting ARR) ÷ starting ARR                                                  | Period, comparable population, and treatment of acquisitions or FX |
| Gross revenue retention | (Starting recurring revenue − contraction − churn) ÷ starting recurring revenue             | Existing-customer cohort; exclude expansion and new customers      |
| Net revenue retention   | (Starting recurring revenue + expansion − contraction − churn) ÷ starting recurring revenue | Same starting cohort and observation period                        |
| CAC                     | Defined acquisition costs ÷ customers acquired                                              | Included costs, channel attribution, and time lag                  |

These ratios require a meaningful, non-zero denominator. For a pre-revenue business, show the underlying evidence and assumptions instead of publishing undefined growth or retention percentages.

ARR is an operating metric. It should not be presented as recognised accounting revenue, contracted cash receipts, or a guaranteed forecast. Subscription changes, usage-based charges, paused contracts, refunds, and multi-currency reporting need explicit rules.

## Explain the revenue bridge

A simple bridge is:

**Ending ARR = starting ARR + new ARR + expansion ARR − contraction ARR − churned ARR**, with separately explained adjustments where needed.

Use consistent definitions across the bridge and the retention calculations. An increase in ARR can coexist with weak retention if new sales compensate for customer losses. Split the movements by segment when the aggregate hides a useful difference.

For customer behaviour, compare cohorts with similar observation windows. Do not treat a customer with two weeks of history as though the full reporting period has elapsed. Small samples deserve an explanation of their limits, rather than a confident churn forecast.

## Connect measures to strategy and cash

For each priority, record a KPI, its source, the accountable owner, the review cadence, and the action a material change would trigger. Show actuals against the approved plan and explain major variances.

Use scenarios when a decision depends on uncertain acquisition, retention, pricing, or hiring assumptions. Cash runway should be based on an explicit cash-flow view. A simple cash divided by average net burn calculation can be a rough indicator, but it hides changes in collection timing, committed spending, and future burn.

## A usable board-pack structure

1. Decision requested and management recommendation.
2. Performance summary: actuals, plan, prior period, and material variances.
3. Revenue quality: bridge, cohorts, retention, and concentration.
4. Growth efficiency: acquisition assumptions, payback analysis, and experiments.
5. Cash and scenarios: assumptions, sensitivities, and funding milestones.
6. Risks, open questions, accountable owners, and next-review actions.

The handover should include the calculation dictionary and data reconciliation, not only the chart. A reader should be able to trace a number back to its source and understand what it excludes.
