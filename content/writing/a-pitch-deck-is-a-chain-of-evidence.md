---
title: 'A pitch deck is a chain of evidence'
date: '2026-11-12'
description: 'How to connect the customer problem, market, traction, and funding ask into a credible investor narrative.'
---

A pitch deck needs a coherent argument. Each section should make the next one easier to believe: this customer has a problem, the proposed solution addresses it, the team has a way to reach that customer, and the funding can unlock a meaningful next milestone.

## Start with a specific customer

Describe who experiences the problem, what they do today, and what that costs them. Distinguish what you have observed from what you still assume. A small set of relevant customer conversations is more useful than a large market number disconnected from the product.

## Size the reachable market

Show the customer count, price or revenue model, and the segment you can realistically serve first. Explain the source and date of each input. A bottom-up model makes assumptions visible and gives an investor something concrete to question.

## Show traction in context

Revenue, usage, repeat purchases, pilots, and letters of intent represent different levels of evidence. Label each accurately. Show the period, denominator, and definition behind growth or retention figures. Keep projections separate from actual results.

## Connect the ask to a milestone

A funding ask becomes clearer when it states what the money pays for, how long the plan runs, and what the business expects to demonstrate by the end. Include a downside case and explain which assumptions matter most.

The model does not need false precision. It needs transparent inputs, a sensible structure, and a way to update the plan when evidence changes.

## Prepare the discussion behind the slides

Keep supporting research, metric definitions, and financial assumptions in a working document. Use investor questions to find gaps in the argument. A polished slide cannot repair an unsupported claim; the underlying work has to hold together.
