An investment assessment should make the case understandable and challengeable. It connects the proposed use of capital to the commercial opportunity, operating assumptions, risks, and intended outcomes. An impact assessment adds a separate question: what change is expected for people or systems, and what evidence could support that claim?

This guide is a framework for analysis and decision preparation. It does not describe a completed investment mandate or an investment recommendation for a particular security.

## Define the mandate before comparing opportunities

Start with the decision-maker's objectives, constraints, investment horizon, available capital, and decision criteria. A proposal can look attractive in isolation while being a poor fit for the organisation's purpose, liquidity needs, or operating capacity.

Record the question the committee must answer. Separate approval to investigate, approval for a pilot, and approval to commit capital. Each stage can require a different level of evidence.

## Build the commercial case

Describe the customer problem, proposed solution, reachable market, business model, and route to market. Explain which statements are observed facts, estimates, forecasts, or untested assumptions. Give each material input a source, date, and owner.

Then test the connection between the narrative and the operating model. Customer acquisition, pricing, retention, delivery capacity, gross margin, and collection timing should support the plan rather than sit in unrelated slides.

## Compare capital and structuring scenarios

Scenario analysis can examine the amount and timing of capital, milestone-based deployment, ownership implications, financing assumptions, and downside exposure. A model should show how these choices affect the organisation's capacity to execute and its future capital requirements.

Keep assumptions visible. Compare a base case with a credible downside case and identify the inputs that most affect the outcome. An attractive headline return is weak evidence if it depends on an unexplained exit value or an unrealistic execution schedule.

Commercial modelling helps prepare the discussion. Legal documents, tax treatment, regulatory requirements, and transaction execution require the relevant qualified advisers.

## Separate outputs from outcomes and impact

| Layer      | Question                                          | Illustrative example                                                          |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Inputs     | What resources are committed?                     | Funding, staff time, equipment                                                |
| Activities | What is delivered?                                | Training sessions or a market-access programme                                |
| Outputs    | What immediate delivery can be counted?           | Sessions completed or participating enterprises                               |
| Outcomes   | What changes for the intended group?              | A measured change in capability, income, or access                            |
| Impact     | What broader change is supported by the evidence? | Sustained improvements, with attention to alternatives and unintended effects |

These examples are illustrative, not claims about a programme I have evaluated. Reporting participation does not, by itself, demonstrate improved income or causal impact.

For each intended outcome, define the population, indicator, baseline, measurement period, data source, collection responsibility, and limitations. Distinguish the organisation's plausible contribution from a causal attribution claim that would require a suitable evaluation design.

For established impact-measurement terminology and metric resources, consult the [Global Impact Investing Network's IRIS+ system](https://iris.thegiin.org/). Select measures that fit the intervention and decision rather than collecting a long list without a purpose.

## Structure the committee brief

A concise investment or project brief can contain:

- The decision requested, strategic fit, and recommendation.
- The commercial thesis and supporting evidence.
- Financial assumptions, structuring options, and downside sensitivities.
- Execution risks, information gaps, and conditions for proceeding.
- Intended impact, measurement design, and the limits of current evidence.
- Monitoring measures, review dates, and accountable owners.

The most useful conclusion states what is known, what remains uncertain, and what evidence would justify the next commitment. That makes the assessment a decision tool rather than a document produced only to secure approval.
